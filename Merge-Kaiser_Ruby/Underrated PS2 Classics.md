**Horror & weird**
- **Rule of Rose**: A creepy, controversial survival horror set in an orphanage. It was banned or pulled in some countries, and copies are now pricey.
- **Kuon**: A atmospheric Heian-era ghost story from FromSoftware, before they were famous for Souls games.

**Action & stylish oddities**
- **Killer7**: Suda51's surreal, rail-ish assassin game with a very divisive style.
- **Mister Mosquito**: You play a mosquito terrorizing a Japanese family. It's exactly as strange as it sounds.
- **Seven Samurai 20XX**: A cel-shaded, sci-fi reimagining of Kurosawa's film.

**RPGs**
- **Digital Devil Saga**: A gritty, underrated Shin Megami Tensei spinoff with a unique story and battle system.
- **Shadow Hearts: Covenant**: A Lovecraftian, quirky RPG set in an alternate WWI, with a timed-ring combat system.
- **Okage: Shadow King**: A dark-comedy RPG where you're a kid possessed by an evil overlord who can't do anything.
- **Radiata Stories**: A charming tri-Ace RPG with 170+ recruitable characters.
- **Steambot Chronicles**: A sandbox RPG about piloting a steampunk mech and playing piano in a seaside town.

**Music & rhythm**
- **Gitaroo Man**: A rhythm game with a wonderfully absurd story and soundtrack.
- **Mad Maestro**: You conduct an orchestra using the analog stick.

Here are some that are weird in a good-natured way:

- **Space Channel 5: Part 2**: Sega's disco-funk reporter fights aliens by copying dance moves. It's relentlessly upbeat and very 60s-space-pop.
- **PaRappa the Rapper 2**: Rhythm gameplay with a rapping dog, a driving-test sequence, and a sunny, silly sense of humor.
- **Chulip**: You play a shy boy who wins over a whole town by kissing people. It's goofy, sweet, and stranger than it sounds.

- **Graffiti Kingdom**: You draw your own creature, and it becomes your fighter. It's a cute, colorful action game that's more fun than its obscurity suggests.
- **Fantavision**: A launch-era PS2 puzzle game where you link up fireworks to set off bigger displays. It's pure fun.

- **Taiko Drum Master**: A Japanese drum rhythm game with a bright, bouncy soundtrack. The special drum controller makes it more fun but isn't required.
- **Klonoa 2: Lunatea's Veil**: A whimsical dream-world platformer with a lovely art style.
- **We Love Katamari**: You probably know the original, but this sequel is the more cheerful, polished pick if you haven't played either.

From my earlier list, **Gitaroo Man**, **Mad Maestro**, and **Mister Mosquito** are also on the cheerful, offbeat side.

Here are some deeper cuts, with a few that are import-only:

- **Doodle Hex** (*Magic Pengel* in Japan): You draw your own creatures with the analog stick and send them into rock-paper-scissors-style battles. It's a close cousin of Graffiti Kingdom, with a storybook look and a lot of charm.
- **Dog's Life**: You play a scrappy dog who herds sheep, sniffs out clues, and tries to rescue his girlfriend from the dog catcher. It's goofy, sincere, and one of the stranger "animal protagonist" games of the era.
- **La Pucelle: Tactics**: An early Nippon Ichi tactical RPG about a demon-hunting teen nun, with a very silly script. It's basically the prototype for Disgaea, with a lighter, stranger tone.
- **Amplitude**: Harmonix's follow-up to Frequency, where you blast through a glowing track by hitting notes across instrument lanes. It's bright, euphoric, and a bit trippy.
- **Napple Tale: Arsia in Daydream Kingdom** (import only): A candy-colored, dreamlike action-adventure with a very Japanese sense of whimsy. It's obscure even among import collectors.
- **Pop'n Music series** (Japan only): Konami's rhythm series with nine big colorful buttons, anime-style characters, and an endlessly bubbly J-pop soundtrack. Several entries came to PS2.

Several of these, especially the import-only ones, are hard to find or have Japanese text, so the Western releases (Doodle Hex, Dog's Life, La Pucelle, Amplitude) are the easiest to start with.
