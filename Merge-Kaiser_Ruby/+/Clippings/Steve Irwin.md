**Stephen Robert Irwin** (22 February 1962 – 4 September 2006) was an Australian [conservationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_movement "Conservation movement"), [environmentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism"), zookeeper, television personality, and wildlife educator. Nicknamed the " **Crocodile Hunter** ", he is regarded as an influential figure in [Australian popular culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Australia "Culture of Australia"),[^1] [^2] and as one of the greatest conservationists of all time.[^3] [^4] [^5]

Irwin grew up around crocodiles and other types of reptiles and was educated regarding them by his father, [Bob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Irwin "Bob Irwin"). He achieved international fame in the late 1990s from the television series *[The Crocodile Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crocodile_Hunter "The Crocodile Hunter")*, an internationally broadcast wildlife documentary series that he co-hosted with his wife, [Terri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terri_Irwin "Terri Irwin"). The couple also hosted the series *[Croc Files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croc_Files "Croc Files")*, *[The Crocodile Hunter Diaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crocodile_Hunter_Diaries "The Crocodile Hunter Diaries")*, and *New Breed Vets*. They also co-owned and operated [Australia Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Zoo "Australia Zoo"), founded by Steve's parents in [Beerwah, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beerwah,_Queensland "Beerwah, Queensland"). They had two children, [Bindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bindi_Irwin "Bindi Irwin") and [Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Irwin_\(conservationist\) "Robert Irwin (conservationist)").

On 4 September 2006, Irwin [died from an injury caused by a stingray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Steve_Irwin "Death of Steve Irwin") while filming [an underwater documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_Deadliest "Ocean's Deadliest") in the [Great Barrier Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Barrier_Reef "Great Barrier Reef"). His death became international news and was met with expressions of shock and grief by fans, the media, governments, and non-profit organisations. Numerous parks, zoos, streets, the vessel [MY *Steve Irwin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MY_Steve_Irwin "MY Steve Irwin"), the snail species *[Crikey steveirwini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crikey_steveirwini "Crikey steveirwini")*, and the asteroid [57567 Crikey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meanings_of_minor-planet_names:_57001%E2%80%9358000#567 "Meanings of minor-planet names: 57001–58000") have been named in his honour. **The Irwin family continues to operate Australia Zoo.**

Stephen Robert Irwin was born on 22 February 1962 to Lyn (d. 2000) and [Bob Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Irwin "Bob Irwin") (b. 1939) in [Upper Ferntree Gully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Ferntree_Gully "Upper Ferntree Gully"), a suburb of [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne").[^6] [^7] His parents were both of Irish descent. His great-grandfather on his father's side, Joseph Irwin, a carpenter born in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin"), immigrated to Australia in 1876, where he married Theresa Byrne, who was also from Dublin.[^8] Lyn was a maternity nurse and Bob is a former plumber and amateur [herpetologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herpetology "Herpetology") who collected reptiles.[^9] Irwin developed an early interest in reptiles, particularly venomous snakes and lizards, often collecting them despite his father's advice. On one occasion, he reportedly brought a [red-bellied black snake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-bellied_black_snake "Red-bellied black snake") home on a public bus, transporting it in the driver's [esky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esky "Esky").[^10]

He moved with his parents as a child to the [Sunshine Coast, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Coast,_Queensland "Sunshine Coast, Queensland") in 1970, where he attended Landsborough State School and Caloundra State High School.[^11] Irwin described his father as a wildlife expert interested in [herpetology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herpetology "Herpetology"), while his mother Lyn was a [wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife "Wildlife") rehabilitator.
After moving to Queensland, Bob and Lyn Irwin started the small [[Queensland Reptile and Fauna Park]], where Steve grew up around [crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodile "Crocodile") and other reptiles.

Irwin became involved with the park in a number of ways, including taking part in daily animal feeding, as well as care and maintenance activities. On his sixth birthday, he was given a 12-foot (4 m) [scrub python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amethystine_python "Amethystine python"). 
He began handling crocodiles at the age of nine after his father had educated him on reptiles from an early age.[^12] Also at age nine, he wrestled his first crocodile, again under his father's supervision.[^13] 
He worked as a volunteer for Queensland's East Coast Crocodile Management program and captured over 100 crocodiles, some of which were relocated, while others were housed at the family park.[^14] Irwin took over the management of the park in 1991 [^14] and renamed it *Australia Zoo* in 1998.[^15] [^16]

## Career

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Steve_irwin_wes_terri.jpg/500px-Steve_irwin_wes_terri.jpg)
> Irwin and [Terri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terri_Irwin "Terri Irwin") with [Wes Mannion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wes_Mannion "Wes Mannion"), the other cast member of *[The Crocodile Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crocodile_Hunter "The Crocodile Hunter")*

Steve and his wife [Terri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terri_Irwin "Terri Irwin") spent their honeymoon trapping crocodiles together. Film footage of their honeymoon, taken by [John Stainton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stainton "John Stainton"), became the first episode of *[The Crocodile Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crocodile_Hunter "The Crocodile Hunter")*. The series debuted on Australian TV screens in 1996 and made its way onto North American television the following year. *The Crocodile Hunter* became successful in the United States, the UK,[^17] and over 130 other countries, reaching 500 million people. Irwin's exuberant and enthusiastic presenting style, broad [Australian accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_English "Australian English"), signature [khaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaki "Khaki") shorts, and [catchphrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_phrase "Catch phrase") 'Crikey!' became known internationally.[^18]

American satellite and cable television channel [Animal Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Planet "Animal Planet") ended *The Crocodile Hunter* with a series finale titled 'Steve's Last Adventure'. The last *Crocodile Hunter* documentary spanned three hours with footage of Irwin's across-the-world adventure in locations including the [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas"), the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River "Yangtze River"), [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo"), and the [Kruger National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruger_National_Park "Kruger National Park").

After *The Crocodile Hunter*, Irwin went on to star in other Animal Planet documentaries, including *[Croc Files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croc_Files "Croc Files")*,[^19] *[The Crocodile Hunter Diaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crocodile_Hunter_Diaries "The Crocodile Hunter Diaries")* [^20] and *New Breed Vets.*[^21] Animal Planet also created the annual *Croc Week* marathon, which lasted a full week in the middle of June, every year from 2000 to 2007.[^22] [^23] [^24] During a January 2006 interview on *The Tonight Show with Jay Leno*, Irwin announced that [Discovery Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Kids "Discovery Kids") would be developing a show for his daughter, [Bindi Sue Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bindi_Irwin "Bindi Irwin") [^25] – a plan realised after his death as the series *[Bindi the Jungle Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bindi_the_Jungle_Girl "Bindi the Jungle Girl")*.[^26]

### Irwin in 2000

In 1998, Irwin continued his television career, working with director [Mark Strickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Strickson "Mark Strickson") to present *The Ten Deadliest Snakes in the World*.[^27] He appeared on several episodes of *[The Tonight Show with Jay Leno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_with_Jay_Leno "The Tonight Show with Jay Leno")*.[^28] [^29] 
A 2000 [FedEx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FedEx "FedEx") commercial with Irwin lightheartedly dealt with the possibility of occupational death from snakebite and the fanciful notion that FedEx would have saved him, if only FedEx were used.[^30]

Under Irwin's leadership, the operations grew to include the zoo, the television series, the Steve Irwin Conservation Foundation (later renamed [Wildlife Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_Warriors "Wildlife Warriors")), and the International Crocodile Rescue. Improvements to the Australia Zoo include the Animal Planet Crocoseum, the rainforest aviary and Tiger Temple. Irwin mentioned that he was considering opening an Australia Zoo in [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas "Las Vegas"), Nevada, and possibly at other sites around the world.[^31]

In 2001, Irwin appeared in a [cameo role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance "Cameo appearance") in the [Eddie Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Murphy "Eddie Murphy") film *[Dr. Dolittle 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Dolittle_2 "Dr. Dolittle 2")*, in which an alligator warns Dolittle that he knows Irwin is going to grab him and is prepared to attack when he does, but Dolittle fails to warn Irwin in time. Irwin's only starring feature film role was in 2002's *[The Crocodile Hunter: Collision Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crocodile_Hunter:_Collision_Course "The Crocodile Hunter: Collision Course")*, which was released to mixed reviews. In the film, Irwin (who portrayed himself and performed numerous stunts) mistakes some [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency") agents for poachers. He sets out to stop them from capturing a crocodile, which, unknown to him, has actually swallowed a [tracking transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracking_transmitter "Tracking transmitter"). The film won the Best Family Feature Film award for a comedy film at the [Young Artist Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Artist_Awards "Young Artist Awards"). The film was produced on a budget of about US$12 million, and has grossed $33 million.[^32] To promote the film, Irwin was featured in an animated short produced by [Animax Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animax_Entertainment "Animax Entertainment") for Intermix.[^33]

In 2002, Irwin and his family appeared in the [Wiggles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wiggles "The Wiggles") video/DVD release *Wiggly Safari*, which was set in Australia Zoo and featured singing and dancing inspired by Australian wildlife.[^34] Irwin fronted an advertising campaign for [The Ghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ghan "The Ghan") in 2003, a passenger train operating between [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide "Adelaide"), [Alice Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Springs,_Northern_Territory "Alice Springs, Northern Territory"), and [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory "Darwin, Northern Territory").[^35] A [Pacific National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_National "Pacific National") [NR class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NR_class "NR class") locomotive was named *Steve Irwin* as part of the campaign.

### Environmentalism

Irwin feeding a [crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodile "Crocodile") at [Australia Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Zoo "Australia Zoo") in 2005

Irwin was a passionate [conservationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_movement "Conservation movement") and believed in promoting [environmentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism") by sharing his excitement about the natural world rather than preaching to people. He was concerned with conservation of [endangered animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered_species "Endangered species") and [land clearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation "Deforestation") leading to loss of habitat. He considered conservation to be the most important part of his work: 'I consider myself a wildlife warrior. My mission is to save the world's endangered species'.[^36] Irwin bought 'large tracts of land' in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"), [Vanuatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanuatu "Vanuatu"), [Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiji "Fiji"), and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), which he described as 'like [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park "National park") ' and stressed the importance of people realising that they could each make a difference.[^37]

Irwin founded the Steve Irwin Conservation Foundation, which became an independent [charity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_\(practice\) "Charity (practice)") and was later renamed ' [Wildlife Warriors Worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_Warriors_Worldwide "Wildlife Warriors Worldwide") '.[^38] He also helped found International Crocodile Rescue,[^39] the Lyn Irwin Memorial Fund (named in memory of his mother, who died in an automobile crash in 2000),[^40] and the Iron Bark Station Wildlife Rehabilitation Facility.[^41]

Irwin urged people to take part in considerate tourism and not support illegal [poaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching "Poaching") through the purchase of items such as turtle shells or [shark-fin soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark-fin_soup "Shark-fin soup").[^42]

[Sir David Attenborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Attenborough "David Attenborough") was an inspiration to Irwin, according to his widow. When presenting a [Lifetime Achievement Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifetime_Achievement_Award "Lifetime Achievement Award") to Attenborough after Irwin's death at the British [National Television Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Television_Awards "National Television Awards") on 31 October 2006, Terri Irwin said, 'If there's one person who directly inspired my husband it's the person being honoured tonight.... \[Steve's\] real, true love was conservation – and the influence of tonight's recipient in preserving the natural world has been immense'.[^43] 
Attenborough reciprocated by praising Irwin for introducing many to the natural world, saying, 'He taught them how wonderful and exciting it was\[;\] he was a born communicator'.[^44]

### Sporting activities

Irwin loved [mixed martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_martial_arts "Mixed martial arts") competitions and trained with Greg Jackson in the fighting/grappling system of [Gaidojutsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaidojutsu "Gaidojutsu").[^45]

He was an avid cricket fan. This was seen during his visit to [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") where he played cricket with some local children and said 'I love cricket' and 'It's a shame we have to go catch some snakes now'. This was seen during the *Crocodile Hunter* episode 'Island of the Snakes'.[^46]

Having grown up in Essendon, Irwin was a fan of the [Essendon Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essendon_Football_Club "Essendon Football Club"), an [Australian rules football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_rules_football "Australian rules football") club in the [Australian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Football_League "Australian Football League").[^47] Irwin took part in an Australian Rules football promotion in Los Angeles as part of 'Australia Week' in early 2006.[^48] After his death, a picture of Irwin wearing a Bombers [guernsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernsey_\(Australian_rules_football\) "Guernsey (Australian rules football)") was shown by [ESPN.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN.com "ESPN.com") in their [Bottom 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN%27s_Bottom_10 "ESPN's Bottom 10") ranking of the worst [Division I FBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Bowl_Subdivision "Football Bowl Subdivision") [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football "College football") teams after Week 1 of the season in tribute to him.[^49]

Having lived in Queensland most of his life, Irwin was also a fan of [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league "Rugby league"). As a teenager, he played for the Caloundra Sharks as a second-rower,[^50] and as an adult he was known to be a passionate [Brisbane Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane_Broncos "Brisbane Broncos") fan and was involved with the club on several occasions. On one occasion after turning up to training he asked if he could tackle the largest player, [Shane Webcke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Webcke "Shane Webcke"). Despite being thrown to the ground and looking like he'd been crushed he was jovial about the experience.

Irwin also supported [rugby union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union "Rugby union"), being a fan of the national team, the [Wallabies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_rugby_union_team "Australia national rugby union team"). He once wore a Wallaby jersey during a demonstration at the zoo. A behind-the-scenes episode of *The Crocodile Hunter* showed Irwin and the crew finding a petrol station in a remote part of [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia") to watch the Wallabies defeat [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_rugby_union_team "France national rugby union team") in the [1999 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Rugby_World_Cup "1999 Rugby World Cup") Final.

Irwin was also a talented surfer.[^51]

### Media campaigns

A poster from Irwin's *Quarantine Matters!* campaign

Irwin was involved in several media campaigns. He enthusiastically joined with the [Australian Quarantine and Inspection Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Quarantine_and_Inspection_Service "Australian Quarantine and Inspection Service") to promote Australia's strict [quarantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarantine "Quarantine") requirements, with advertisements and posters featuring slogans such as, 'Quarantine Matters! Don't muck with it'. His payments for these advertising campaigns were directed into his wildlife fund.[^52]

In 2004, Irwin was appointed ambassador for [The Ghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ghan "The Ghan"), the passenger train running from [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide "Adelaide") to [Alice Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Springs "Alice Springs") in the central Australian outback, when the line was extended all the way to [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory "Darwin, Northern Territory") on the northern coast that year. For some time he was sponsored by [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota "Toyota").[^53]

Irwin was a keen promoter for Australian tourism in general and Queensland tourism in particular. In 2002, the Australia Zoo was voted Queensland's top tourist attraction.[^36] His immense popularity in the United States meant he often promoted Australia as a tourist destination there.[^54] As a part of the United States' 'Australia Week' celebrations in January 2006, Irwin appeared at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA "UCLA") 's [Pauley Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauley_Pavilion "Pauley Pavilion") in Los Angeles, California.[^55]

In November 2003, Irwin was filming a documentary on sea lions off the coast of [Baja California Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_California_Peninsula "Baja California Peninsula") in Mexico when he heard via his boat's radio that two [scuba divers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuba_diver "Scuba diver") were reported missing in the area. Irwin and his entire crew suspended operations to aid in the search. His team's divers searched with the rescue divers, and Irwin used his vessel to patrol the waters around the island where the incident occurred, as well as using his satellite communications system to call in a rescue plane. On the second day of the search, [kayakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayaker "Kayaker") found one of the divers, Scott Jones, perched on a narrow rock ledge jutting out from the side of a cliff. Irwin and a crew member escorted him to Irwin's boat. The other lost diver, Katie Vrooman, was found dead by a search plane later the same day not far from Jones' location.[^56]

Irwin died on 4 September 2006, after being pierced in the chest by a [short-tail stingray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-tail_stingray "Short-tail stingray") barb while filming in the [Great Barrier Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Barrier_Reef "Great Barrier Reef") with [Philippe Cousteau Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Cousteau_Jr. "Philippe Cousteau Jr.") The stinger penetrated his [thoracic wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_wall "Thoracic wall"), causing massive trauma.[^57] [^58] He was at [Batt Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batt_Reef "Batt Reef"), near [Port Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Douglas "Port Douglas"), Queensland, taking part in the production of the documentary series *[Ocean's Deadliest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_Deadliest "Ocean's Deadliest")*. Irwin's death is believed to be the only fatality from a stingray captured on video.[^59] His death was met with shock and grief by fans, the media, governments, and non-profit organisations.

A private funeral service took place on 9 September 2006.[^60] Irwin was buried in a private ceremony at [Australia Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Zoo "Australia Zoo") later that same day.[^61] A public memorial service was held in Australia Zoo's 5,500-seat Crocoseum on 30 September 2006; this service was broadcast live and it is estimated to have been seen by over 300 million viewers.[^62]

Flowers left in Irwin's memory the day after his death

In 1992, Irwin met [Terri Raines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terri_Irwin "Terri Irwin"), an American naturalist from [Eugene, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon "Eugene, Oregon"), who was visiting wildlife rehabilitation facilities in Australia and had decided to visit the zoo. According to the couple, it was love at first sight.[^63] Terri said at the time, 'I thought there was no one like this anywhere in the world. He sounded like an environmental Tarzan, a larger-than-life superhero guy'.[^64] They were engaged four months later and were married at the [First United Methodist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Methodist_Church "United Methodist Church") of Eugene on 4 June 1992.[^65] Together they had two children: a daughter, [Bindi Sue Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bindi_Irwin "Bindi Irwin") (born 24 July 1998), and a son, [Robert Clarence Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Irwin_\(conservationist\) "Robert Irwin (conservationist)") (born 1 December 2003).[^66] Bindi Sue is jointly named after two of Steve Irwin's favourite animals: Bindi, a [saltwater crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_crocodile "Saltwater crocodile"), and Sui, a [Staffordshire Bull Terrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_Bull_Terrier "Staffordshire Bull Terrier"). Robert is named after Irwin's father Bob and Terri's father Clarence.

Irwin once described his daughter Bindi as 'the reason \[he\] was put on the Earth'. His wife once said, 'The only thing that could ever keep him away from the animals he loves are the people he loves even more'.[^31] Although the Irwins were happily married, they did not wear wedding rings; they believed that in their line of work, wearing jewellery could pose a hazard to them or the animals.[^67] Irwin frequently said that if he was to be remembered for anything, he hoped that it would be for being a good father.[^6]

On 11 February 2000, Irwin's mother Lyn was killed in a car accident. In an online tribute, he called her 'the most beautiful, loving, nurturing, and caring person to have ever blessed this world'.[^68]

## Controversies

On 2 January 2004, Irwin carried his one-month-old son, [Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Irwin_\(conservationist\) "Robert Irwin (conservationist)"), in his arm while hand-feeding a chicken carcass to Murray, a 3.8-metre (12 ft 6 in) saltwater crocodile. The infant was close to the crocodile, and comparisons were made in the press to [Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson "Michael Jackson") dangling his son outside a German hotel window.[^69] In addition, some child welfare groups, [animal rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_rights "Animal rights") groups, and some of Irwin's television viewers criticised his actions as irresponsible and tantamount to [child abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_abuse "Child abuse").[^70] Irwin apologised on the US NBC show *[Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_\(U.S._TV_program\) "Today (U.S. TV program)")*.[^71] Both he and his wife publicly stated that Irwin was in complete control of the situation, as he had dealt with crocodiles since he was a small child, and based on his lifetime of experience neither he nor his son were in any danger. He also showed footage of the event shot from a different angle, demonstrating that they were much farther from the crocodile than they had appeared in the publicised clip.[^72] Terri said their child was in no more danger than one being taught to swim. No charges were filed; according to one journalist, Irwin told officials he would not repeat the action.[^73] The incident prompted the [Queensland Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Government "Queensland Government") to change its crocodile-handling laws, banning children and untrained adults from entering crocodile enclosures.[^74]

In June 2004, allegations were made that he disturbed wildlife (namely whales, seals and penguins) while filming *The Crocodile Hunter* episode " [Ice Breaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Breaker_\(The_Crocodile_Hunter\) "Ice Breaker (The Crocodile Hunter)") " in [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica"). The matter was subsequently closed without charges being laid.[^75]

After questions arose in 2003 about Irwin being paid $175,000 worth of taxpayers' money to appear in a television advertisement and his possible political ties, Irwin told the [Australian Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Broadcasting_Corporation "Australian Broadcasting Corporation") (ABC) that he was a conservationist and did not choose sides in politics. His comments describing Australian Prime Minister [John Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Howard "John Howard") as the 'greatest leader in the world' earned him scorn in the media.[^76]

In response to questions of Australia's problems with [overgrazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overgrazing "Overgrazing"), [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity_in_Australia "Salinity in Australia"), and [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion "Erosion"), Irwin responded: "Cows have been on our land for so long that Australia has evolved to handle those big animals".[^77] *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald "The Sydney Morning Herald")* concluded with the opinion that his message was confusing and amounted to "eating roos and crocs is bad for tourism, and more cruel than eating other animals".[^77]

## Legacy

Irwin provided his voice for the [animated film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animated_film "Animated film") *[Happy Feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Feet "Happy Feet")*, as an [elephant seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_elephant_seal "Southern elephant seal") named Trev. The film was posthumously released in November 2006, and it was dedicated to Irwin, as he died during post-production two months prior.[^78] Another, previously incomplete scene, featuring Irwin providing the voice of an albatross and essentially playing himself, was restored to the DVD release.[^79]

In 2007, a special episode of *The Crocodile Hunter* was made in tribute to him; *[Crikey! What an Adventure: An Intimate Look at the Life of Steve Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_The_Crocodile_Hunter_episodes#Specials "List of The Crocodile Hunter episodes").* The documentary features archive footage from *The Crocodile Hunter*. Later that year, Bindi released the documentary *[My Daddy, the Crocodile Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Daddy,_the_Crocodile_Hunter "My Daddy, the Crocodile Hunter")* in Irwin's memory. He appears in several episodes of *Bindi the Jungle Girl* via archive footage. Archive footage of him has also been used in the television series *[Crikey! It's the Irwins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crikey!_It%27s_the_Irwins "Crikey! It's the Irwins"),* which began airing in 2018.

Steve Irwin Day is an annual event celebrated at [Australia Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Zoo "Australia Zoo") on 15 November, honouring the life and legacy of Irwin. The date was chosen because it takes place on the birthday of one of Irwin's favourite animals, a [tortoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortoise "Tortoise") from the [Galápagos Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_Islands "Galápagos Islands").[^80] Events that take place include people raising money for [Wildlife Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_Warriors "Wildlife Warriors") to help continue Irwin's conservation work,[^81] [^82] [^83] and employees at [Australia Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Zoo "Australia Zoo") wearing khaki uniforms in Irwin's memory.[^81] [^83] [^84] [^85]

### Honours

Steve Irwin Memorial, Mooloolaba, Queensland

In 1997, while on a fishing trip on the coast of [Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland "Queensland") with his father, Irwin discovered a new species of turtle. Herpetologist [John Cann](https://species.wikimedia.org/wiki/John_Cann "species:John Cann") named it [Irwin's turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irwin%27s_turtle "Irwin's turtle") (*[Elseya irwini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elseya_irwini "Elseya irwini"))* in honour of Steve Irwin.[^86] [^87] Another newly discovered Australian animal – a species of air-breathing [land snail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_snail "Land snail"), *[Crikey steveirwini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crikey_steveirwini "Crikey steveirwini")*, was named after Irwin in 2009.[^88] In 2025, a species of [snake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake "Snake") native to [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") 's [Nicobar Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicobar_Islands "Nicobar Islands"), *[Lycodon irwini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycodon_irwini "Lycodon irwini")*, was named in his honour.

Irwin was awarded the [Centenary Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenary_Medal "Centenary Medal") by the Australian government in 2001 for his 'service to global conservation and to Australian tourism'.[^89] In 2004, he was recognised as Tourism Export of the Year.[^90] He was also nominated in 2004 for [Australian of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_of_the_Year "Australian of the Year") but it was awarded to Australian cricket captain [Steve Waugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Waugh "Steve Waugh"),[^91] while Irwin was named 2004 Queensland Australian of the Year.[^92] Shortly before his death, Irwin was to be named an adjunct professor at the [University of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Queensland "University of Queensland") 's School of Integrative Biology. On 14 November 2007, Irwin was awarded the adjunct professorship posthumously.[^93] In 2007, Irwin was posthumously inducted into the [Logie Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logie_Hall_of_Fame "Logie Hall of Fame").[^94]

In May 2007, the government of [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda "Rwanda") announced that it would name a baby gorilla after Irwin as a tribute to his work in wildlife conservation.[^95] Also in 2007, the state government of [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala "Kerala"), India named the Crocodile Rehabilitation and Research Centre at [Neyyar Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neyyar_Wildlife_Sanctuary "Neyyar Wildlife Sanctuary") in his honour;[^96] however, Terri objected that this action had been taken without her permission and asked the Kerala government in 2009 to stop using Irwin's name and images [^97] – a request with which the state government complied in mid-2009.[^98]

Irwin, after his death, was described by Mark Townend, CEO of [RSPCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSPCA_Australia "RSPCA Australia") Queensland, as a 'modern-day [Noah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah "Noah") '.[^99] British naturalist [David Bellamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bellamy "David Bellamy") lauded his skills as a [natural historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history "Natural history") and media performer.[^100] Canadian environmentalist [David Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Suzuki "David Suzuki") paid tribute to Irwin, noting that '\[h\]umanity will not protect that which we fear or do not understand. Steve Irwin helped us understand those things that many people thought were a nuisance at best, a horror at worst. That made him a great educator and conservationist'.[^101]

After his death, the vessel [MV *Robert Hunter*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MY_Steve_Irwin "MY Steve Irwin") owned by the environmental action group [Sea Shepherd Conservation Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Shepherd_Conservation_Society "Sea Shepherd Conservation Society") was renamed [MY *Steve Irwin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MY_Steve_Irwin "MY Steve Irwin").[^102] Shortly before his death, Irwin had been investigating joining Sea Shepherd's 2007–2008 voyage to Antarctica to disrupt Japanese whaling activity. Following his death, the organisation suggested renaming their vessel, and this idea was endorsed by Terri.[^103] Regarding the ship and its new name, Terri said, 'If Steve were alive, he'd be aboard with them!' [^104]

Irwin was inducted in 2009 into the [Queensland Business Leaders Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Business_Leaders_Hall_of_Fame "Queensland Business Leaders Hall of Fame"), recognised for international entrepreneurship both in business and wildlife conservation, significantly contributing to Queensland and its international reputation [^105] and in 2015, Irwin was a posthumous recipient of the [Queensland Greats Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Greats_Awards "Queensland Greats Awards").[^106] In 2017 it was announced that Irwin would be posthumously honoured with a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame").[^107] [^108] The star was unveiled on 26 April 2018.[^109] On 22 February 2019, the 57th anniversary of Irwin's birth, the search engine Google released a [Google Doodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Doodle "Google Doodle") commemorating him, in the form of a slideshow.[^110]

### Within Australia

In the years after his death, Irwin's likeness, catchphrases, mannerisms, and contributions have made Steve Irwin synonymous with Australia among both citizens and foreigners. His likeness has been used by the [Queensland Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Government "Queensland Government") to promote tourism in the state with them regarding Australia Zoo as one of the state's most iconic tourist destinations.[^111] His son [Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Irwin_\(conservationist\) "Robert Irwin (conservationist)") became a spokesperson for Queensland's tourism board in 2020.[^112]

Glass House Mountains Road, which runs along the front of Australia Zoo, was also renamed [Steve Irwin Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Irwin_Way "Steve Irwin Way") by the Queensland Government as a tribute to Irwin. The renaming was announced three weeks after Irwin's death.[^113]

In 2022, following the death of [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II "Queen Elizabeth II"), some Australians voiced support for the nation's [currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_dollar "Australian dollar") to feature Irwin instead of [King Charles III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Charles_III "King Charles III"), with two citizens filing separate petitions to gather support for the cause.[^114] When asked by the press on the matter, prime minister [Anthony Albanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Albanese "Anthony Albanese") declined to take a position, offering that it wasn't the right time to discuss who would be on the currency in the immediate wake of the Queen's death.[^115]
