Sony’s [PlayStation](http://www.cbr.com/tag/playstation/) is now a top performer in the gaming industry, but it faced an uphill battle when it went up against Nintendo and Sega when the original console made its debut in 1994. Sony’s PlayStation was a game-changing console that effectively shook up the console wars by introducing revolutionary new controls, hardware, and franchises. The original PlayStation had a lifespan of over a decade and a staggering total of more than 8000 games. Sony’s PlayStation confidently held its own against the Sega Saturn and the Nintendo 64, plus there would be no PlayStation 5 today without the trail that it blazed back in the ‘90s.

The PlayStation has many unforgettable titles that were some of the biggest gaming sensations of the ‘90s, such as *Gran Turismo, Final Fantasy VII, Metal Gear Solid, Silent Hill*, and the original *Resident Evil* trilogy. However, there are many other cutting-edge games that have slipped through the cracks and fallen into obscurity, even though they deserve just as much love as the PlayStation’s bestsellers.

## 15 [[Omega Boost]] Is A High-Octane Rail Shooter That Delivers Non-Stop Action

### Impressive Visuals and Intense Combat Keep Players on the Edge of Their Seats

![Two mechs fighting in the sky](https://static0.cbrimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Omega-Boost-Cropped.jpg?q=70&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=1)

Image via Sony

*Omega Boost* is one of the most adrenaline-pumping titles on the PlayStation, offering a rail-shooter experience unlike anything else on the system. Developed by Polyphony Digital, the creators of the *Gran Turismo* series, *Omega Boost* makes full use of the PlayStation's hardware to deliver fast-paced space combat in a visually impressive package. Players pilot the Omega Boost mecha through various stages, battling waves of enemies and massive bosses in thrilling zero-gravity environments. **The seamless blend of smooth controls, cinematic action, and explosive set pieces** creates an exhilarating experience that's hard to put down.

In addition to its breathtaking visuals and high-octane combat, *Omega Boost* offers a surprising depth with its complex upgrade system. Players can customize their mecha with new weapons and abilities, adding a layer of strategy to the chaotic action. The game's difficulty ramps up quickly, providing a challenge even for seasoned players. Despite its visual and gameplay excellence, *Omega Boost* remained relatively underappreciated, likely overshadowed by Polyphony Digital's more popular *Gran Turismo* series. However, for space shooters and mecha combat fans, *Omega Boost* remains a hidden gem worth rediscovering.

## 14 R-Type Delta Enhances Classic Sidescrolling Action With Beautiful 3D Graphics

### Tactical Gameplay and Boss Battles Challenge Even Seasoned Players

![R-Type Delta boss battle against a metal arm](https://static0.cbrimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/r-type-delta.jpg?q=70&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=1)

R-Type Delta boss battle against a metal arm

*R-Type Delta* is a masterful evolution of the *R-Type* franchise, taking the classic sidescrolling shooter into the realm of 3D while maintaining the signature difficulty that fans have come to expect. As the first *R-Type* game to feature polygonal graphics, *Delta* pushes the PlayStation hardware to deliver visually stunning environments and intricate enemy designs. **The transition to 3D allows for greater environmental complexity**, with stages that evolve dynamically and add new layers of challenge as players progress.

The gameplay remains rooted in the series' tactical approach to shooting, where the right combination of weapons and strategic positioning is key to survival. *R-Type Delta* features multiple playable ships, each with its own unique set of weapons and abilities, offering a variety of playstyles. The game's intense boss battles are a highlight, forcing players to memorize attack patterns and react with precision. *R-Type Delta* also incorporates a branching path system, encouraging multiple playthroughs to experience its content. Though it never reached the popularity of other shooters, *R-Type Delta* remains a high point in the genre and a must-play for fans of challenging, tactical action.

## 13 [[Klonoa Door to Phantomile]] Mixes 2D Gameplay With 3D Aesthetics To Create a Whimsical Adventure

### Emotional Storytelling and Creative Level Design Set This Platformer Apart

![Screenshot depicting gameplay in Klonoa: Door to Phantomile, as seen in Klonoa Phantasy Reverie Series.](https://static0.cbrimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Klonoa-Phantasy-Reverie-Series-Phantomile-Gameplay.jpg?q=49&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=2)

*Klonoa: Door to Phantomile* is a delightful platformer that seamlessly blends 2D side-scrolling gameplay with beautifully rendered 3D environments, creating a unique visual style that still holds up today. Players take control of Klonoa, a young hero who embarks on a journey to save the world of Phantomile from an encroaching darkness. The game's core mechanic involves using Klonoa's ring to grab enemies and use them as projectiles to solve puzzles or reach new areas. **This inventive gameplay mechanic sets *Klonoa* apart from other platformers** and adds a layer of strategy to the otherwise straightforward action.

However, *Klonoa's* appeal extends beyond its mechanics. The game's story is surprisingly emotional, with a narrative that explores themes of dreams, friendship, and sacrifice. As Klonoa ventures through the vibrant worlds of Phantomile, players are treated to a series of beautifully designed levels that make excellent use of the game's 2.5D perspective. While the game was overshadowed by more popular platformers like *Crash Bandicoot* and *Spyro the Dragon*, *Klonoa* has earned a cult following for its heartfelt story and creative gameplay. It's a game that resonates emotionally with players, making it one of the most memorable experiences on the PlayStation.

PS1

## 12 Bushido Blade Redefines Fighting Games With Realistic Sword Combat and No Health Bars

### Tension Builds From the Innovative One-Hit Kill Mechanic


![Red Shadow slashes at Mikado with their sword in Bushido Blade|322](https://static0.cbrimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Bushido-Blade.jpg?q=49&fit=crop&w=300&h=273&dpr=2)

![Bushido Blade 1093 8](https://static0.cbrimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Bushido-Blade-1093-8.jpg?q=49&fit=crop&w=146&h=120&dpr=2)

*Bushido Blade* is an innovative fighting game that completely rethinks the genre's core mechanics, focusing on realism and strategy rather than combos and health bars. Set in feudal Japan, players take control of samurai and engage in intense sword duels where a single well-placed strike can result in an instant victory or defeat. **This unique one-hit kill system adds tension and excitement** that few other fighting games can match. The game's environments, which range from peaceful gardens to dark bamboo forests, add to the atmosphere and can be used strategically during battles.

Its attention to detail and commitment to realism make Bushido Blade truly special. In addition to the one-hit kill mechanic, players can injure specific limbs, which can cripple their opponent and dramatically change the flow of the fight. The game also features a first-person mode, immersing players in the action. *Bushido Blade's* slower, more deliberate combat may not have resonated with fans of faster-paced fighters like *Tekken*, but it gained a loyal following for its unique take on the genre. Its influence can still be felt in modern games focusing on realistic combat mechanics.

## 11 The Misadventures of Tron Bonne Mixes Puzzle Solving With Action In A Spin-Off That Stands On Its Own

### Unique Mechanics and Lighthearted Storytelling Bring Charm to the Series

![Tron Bonne helps rob a bank in the PlayStation's The Misadventures of Tron Bonne](https://static0.cbrimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/the-misadventures-of-tron-bonne-bank-robbery-playstation.jpg?q=70&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=1)

Tron Bonne helps rob a bank in the PlayStation's The Misadventures of Tron Bonne

*The Misadventures of Tron Bonne* is a charming spin-off from the *Mega Man Legends* series. Players control the mischievous Tron Bonne as she embarks on a series of heists to save her family. Unlike traditional *Mega Man* games, *Tron Bonne* mixes action, puzzle-solving, and strategy, creating a gameplay experience full of variety. **The game's inventive mechanics, including controlling Tron's Servbots to solve puzzles and execute heists**, keep the gameplay fresh and engaging across the different levels.

Each mission offers a unique challenge, from infiltrating secure vaults to engaging in mecha battles, and the game's lighthearted tone makes it a joy to play. The Servbots add an extra layer of charm, as they can be trained and customized to assist in missions, adding a strategic element to the game. *Tron Bonne's* quirky humor and playful narrative distinguish it from other action-adventure games of the era. While it may not have the widespread recognition of other *Mega Man* titles, it has garnered a dedicated fanbase for its unique gameplay and lovable characters.

**Platform(s)**

PS1, PS3

**Genre(s)**

Action-Adventure

**How Long To Beat**

8 Hours

The rhythm genre was still in its infancy during the PlayStation era, but the Sony console helped legitimize it in some major ways. *Vib-Ribbon* has a bare-bones look that features white vector lines instead of rich graphics or detailed FMV sequences. That being said, it's one of the console's most entertaining titles and contains an essentially infinite number of levels. *Vib-Ribbon* curiously started as a dropped Mercedes-Benz ad that continued development into a full-fledged game. *Vib-Ribbon* **features a bunny mascot known as Vibri who walks down paths and avoids obstacles**, all of which are generated by unique musical tracks.

*Vib-Ribbon* has plenty of its own generated levels, but it can also play music CDs that create fresh courses based on the musical tracks. The tempo and complexity of the music dictate the level's challenge. It's an incredibly inventive game that was led by *PaRappa the Rapper* and *Um Jammer Lammy* producer, Masaya Matsuura. *Vib-Ribbon* found more success in Japan with two follow-up games — *Mojib-Ribbon* and the PS2's *Vib-Ripple* — but it's considerably more obscure in North America and Europe.

## 9 Heart Of Darkness Is A Perilous Cinematic Platformer Where A Boy Strives To Survive Against Shadow Monsters

**Stunning Visuals and Emotional Storytelling Create a Memorable Gaming Experience**

![Shadow creatures attack in PlayStation's Heart of Darkness.|363](https://static0.cbrimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/PS1-Heart-Of-Darkness.jpg?q=70&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=1)
> Shadow creatures attack in PlayStation's Heart of Darkness.

*Heart of Darkness* shoots for the moon and the technically impressive "cinematic platformer" had a lengthy development cycle of six years and a huge budget that allowed for many perks that were rare or unheard of at the time, such as a full orchestral musical score and extensive FMV story cutscenes. *Heart of Darkness* follows Andy, a young boy who's afraid of the dark, but determined to rescue his missing dog from evil shadow monsters. The game’s narrative is deeply engaging, with players finding themselves emotionally invested in Andy's quest and the perilous situations he encounters.

*Heart of Darkness* is highly innovative and features a surprising **number of ways that Andy can perish as the player solves players and works their way through** the whimsical and foreboding experience. *Heart of Darkness* is a short game, which held it back from finding mainstream success, but everything that it does is beautifully implemented. It's a satisfying spiritual successor to Éric Chahi's previous gaming gem, *Another World*. The game has since gained recognition for its artistic merits and has influenced many modern indie titles that strive for emotional depth and visual flair.

## 8 Incredible Crisis Is A Surreal Genre-Bending Mini-Game Extravaganza

### A Diverse Array of Mini-Games Combines Humor, Fun, and Unpredictability

![Tahou balances in mini-game action in PlayStation's Incredible Crisis.](https://static0.cbrimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/incredible-crisis-feature-2.jpg?q=70&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=1)

Image via Titus Interactive

*Incredible Crisis* is one of the PlayStation's strangest relics, and it's quite surprising that it even received localized releases outside of Japan. *Incredible Crisis* is an unusual collection of diverse mini-games that are connected through an ongoing action story where the Tanamatsuri family prepares for the family's grandmother's birthday. **Twenty-four mini-games are divided among the family's four members** as they each go about their days, manage their stress levels, and properly prepare for Hatsu's birthday.

These mini-games range from rhythm-based dance exercises to trivia contests to action-centric obstacle courses. *Incredible Crisis* may not be super challenging. That being said, it's unforgettable and a game that's truly one-of-a-kind during a time when derivative and lazy copycat releases could dominate the market. The game has also been praised for its humor and its ability to capture the essence of Japanese culture, making it a unique gem in the PlayStation library.

## 7 Jumping Flash! & Jumping Flash! 2 Are Formative 3D Platformers That Changed The Game

### Groundbreaking 3D Gameplay and Inventive Level Design Redefine Platforming

![Robbit looks over the beach world from high above in PlayStation's Jumping Flash!](https://static0.cbrimages.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Jumping-Flash-Cropped.jpg?q=70&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=1)

Robbit looks over the beach world from high above in PlayStation's Jumping Flash!

Some of the PlayStation's best games are from its later years, but the success of *Jumping Flash!* — a launch title — is a testament to the console's confident entry into the gaming industry. Players control a robotic rabbit named Robbit in what's technically the first 3D platformer. *Jumping Flash!* **creatively mixes platforming with FPS elements**, as well as item collecting and creative boss battles, in a peculiar package that somehow works. This innovative combination not only set a precedent for future platformers but also showcased the potential of 3D gameplay in a way that had never been done before.

It's easy to focus on the gaming generation's more sophisticated 3D platformers, like *Super Mario 64*, *Crash Bandicoot*, and *Spyro the Dragon*, but *Jumping Flash!* is still remarkably charming and takes advantage of its retro polygonal aesthetic. *Jumping Flash!* — as well as its sequel from the following year, *Jumping Flash! 2* — is an innovative piece of gaming history that's often forgotten.

## 6 Tobal No. 1 Is An Original PS1 Fighter That Takes Big Risks

### Fluid Combat and Engaging Adventure Mode Offer Depth Beyond Traditional Fighting

The PlayStation was a great console for fighting games, including many arcade ports from celebrated franchises like *Tekken*. *Tobal No. 1* was designed to be an original home console fighter and it recruited [Akira Toriyama of *Dragon Ball* fame](https://www.cbr.com/how-akira-toriyama-changed-anime/) to design all its characters. *Tobal No. 1* benefits from fluid 60 frames per second gameplay and a creative adventure mode that mixes the title's **fighting mechanics into an RPG-esque dungeon quest** that's done to unlock new characters. This concept was revolutionary at the time, and it's still incredibly innovative.

There's a rich character roster in *Tobal No. 1* where no one feels redundant, and it's also significant for being Square's first PlayStation title. *Tobal No. 1* is often remembered for including a playable *Final Fantasy VII* demo, but it deserves to stand on its own and a modern re-release is long overdue. *Tobal 2* is equally exciting, and some audiences even prefer it to its predecessor due to its expanded 200-character roster, but it unfortunately was never released outside of Japan.
