**Rebecca J. Wirfs-Brock** (born 1953 in [Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon "Portland, Oregon"), [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon "Oregon")) is an American software engineer and consultant in [object-oriented programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object-oriented_programming "Object-oriented programming") and [object-oriented design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object-oriented_design "Object-oriented design"), the founder of the [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology "Information technology") consulting firm Wirfs-Brock Associates, and inventor of [Responsibility-Driven Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsibility-Driven_Design "Responsibility-Driven Design"), the first behavioral approach to object design.

Wirfs-Brock holds a B.A. in [computer and information science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon_Department_of_Computer_and_Information_Science "University of Oregon Department of Computer and Information Science") and **psychology** from the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon "University of Oregon").[^1] She worked at [[ektronix]] for 15 years as a software engineer before moving on to [[Instantiations]] (founded by her husband Allen Wirfs-Brock), which was acquired by [[Digitalk]] which merged with Parc Place Systems to become [[ParcPlace-Digitalk]] in 1995. She was the Chief Technologist for the professional services organization of a [Smalltalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smalltalk "Smalltalk") language vendor.

She holds a U.S. Patent #4,635,049 "Apparatus for Presenting Image Information for Display Graphically" together with [[Warren Dodge]].

Wirfs-Brock first coined the "-driven" meme in an [OOPSLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OOPSLA "OOPSLA") 1989 paper she co-authored with [[Brian Wilkerson]].[^2] Before that time, the most prevalent way of structuring objects was based on [entity-relationship modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entity-relationship_model "Entity-relationship model") ideas (popularized by [James Rumbaugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Rumbaugh "James Rumbaugh"), [Steve Mellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_J._Mellor "Stephen J. Mellor") and [Sally Shlaer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Shlaer "Sally Shlaer")).

She wrote about object role stereotypes in 1992 in a Smalltalk Report article and this influenced the [UML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Modeling_Language "Unified Modeling Language") notion of stereotypes. Her invention of the conversational (two-column) form of [use cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_case "Use case") was then popularized by [Larry Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Constantine "Larry Constantine"). Most of the more recent "driven" design approaches acknowledge their roots and the influence of RDD, of which [class-responsibility-collaboration cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class-responsibility-collaboration_card "Class-responsibility-collaboration card") are one popular technique. She was the design columnist for *[IEEE Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Software "IEEE Software")* until December 2009.
