The **reed switch** <sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-NB_Herkon-1"><span>[</span>i<span>]</span></a></sup> is an [electromechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromechanics "Electromechanics") [switch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switch "Switch") operated by an applied [magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field "Magnetic field"). It was invented in 1922 by professor Valentin Kovalenkov at the [Petrograd Electrotechnical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great_St._Petersburg_Polytechnic_University#Soviet_era "Peter the Great St. Petersburg Polytechnic University"),<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-IEEE_Reed_relay_history-2"><span>[</span>1<span>]</span></a></sup> and later evolved at [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs") in 1936 by Walter B. Ellwood into the reed relay. 

In its simplest and most common form, it consists of a pair of [ferromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism "Ferromagnetism") flexible metal [contacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_contacts "Electrical contacts") in a [hermetically sealed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermetic_seal "Hermetic seal") [glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass "Glass") envelope. The contacts are usually [normally open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normally_open "Normally open"), closing when a magnetic field is present, or they may be normally closed and open when a magnetic field is applied. The switch may be actuated by an [electromagnetic coil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_coil "Electromagnetic coil"), making a [reed relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_relay "Reed relay"),<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Graf_1975-3"><span>[</span>2<span>]</span></a></sup> or by bringing a permanent [magnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet "Magnet") near it. When the magnetic field is removed, the contacts in the reed switch return to their original position. The "reed" is the metal part inside the reed switch envelope that is relatively thin and wide to make it flexible, resembling the [reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_\(mouthpiece\) "Reed (mouthpiece)") of a [musical instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_instruments "Musical instruments"). The term "reed" may also include the external wire lead as well as the internal part.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-NB_Herkon-1"><span>[</span>i<span>]</span></a></sup>
