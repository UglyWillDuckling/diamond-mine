A laptop’s **hard drive** is one of its primary components; it stores all of your laptop’s digital data, including OS-related files, system and user files, and so much more. Hard drives are directly connected to the motherboard via an Advanced Technology Attachment (ATA), parallel ATA, or Small Computer System Interface (SCSI) cable, among other cable connections. Hard drives are also called **hard disk drives**  or **HDDs**.

## How to Hook Up Laptop Hard Drive to Desktop

Hard drives that are found in most laptops can be divided into three kinds: **Integrated Drive Electronics (IDE)**, **Serial Advanced Technology Attachment (SATA)**, and **Solid State Drive (SSD)**. SATA hard drives are commonly found on most older and newer laptop models. However, SSDs are a great upgrade and are being used on newer and flagship laptop brands.

So, if you’ve decided to open your laptop’s internals, chances are you’re going to find either a SATA or SSD hard drive. But the question is, can a laptop hard drive **work** on a desktop? Here are some techniques for connecting your laptop’s hard drive to a desktop computer.

### Method #1: Connect Hard Drive Directly to the PC’s Motherboard

You can try this method if you have a desktop setup that **supports** two hard drives inside its casing. Also, laptop hard drives are typically **smaller** than your desktop’s hard drives. So you’ll need to have a **mounting bracket** that securely supports it to avoid it from sliding out of place.

**Important**: Keep **magnets** away from your laptop’s hard drive to avoid damage and data loss.

To connect your laptop hard drive directly to the PC’s motherboard, follow these steps:

#### Step 1: Removing your laptop’s hard drive

1. **Turn off** your laptop and **unplug** it from any power source—if it has a removable battery, **detach it** from your laptop.

**Note**: If your laptop’s battery is built-in internally, you’ll need to **take it off first** before removing other components.

1. Press the **Power** button a few times to ensure that all power is **drained** from the laptop’s internal components.
2. Next, remove **all screws** that are keeping the laptop’s back casing in place. Since most laptops have **very small screws**, you can use a **jeweler’s screwdriver** to remove them.
3. After removing the laptop’s CD/DVD drive and keyboard, you will now have a clear view of where the **hard drive** is—it’s typically at the **bottom-left corner** of the laptop and is held by a few screws. Remove it by **sliding** it outside.

#### Step 2: Connecting your hard drive to the desktop

1. To connect your hard drive to the motherboard, you’ll need to have an **adapter** or **connector** that’s compatible with your hard drive. If you have a SATA hard drive, you can purchase a **SATA connector** online or at a tech store.
2. **Turn off** your PC and disconnect it from any **power source**.
3. Open it and find the **motherboard**; it should have spare **SATA sockets** available.
4. Connect the **SATA connector** to your hard drive and plug it into one of the **sockets**.
5. Next, **mount** the hard drive to a secure bracket to keep it in place.
6. Reassemble your PC and boot it up to **BIOS**; check the connected hard drive and see if it’s working.

### Method #2: Connect Hard Drive to PC via USB

If your PC’s **USB ports** are in good condition, it’s more convenient to connect your hard drive **via USB connection**. This way, you won’t need to **open** your PC’s housing and connect the hard drive yourself. Also, you can choose between various methods that are all easy to do.

#### Via Adapters

One way of connecting your hard drive to a desktop is through **USB adapters**. This is ideal if your PC’s USB ports are running **USB 3.0** since it can quickly transfer data from your hard drive to your PC. However, most adapters still use power so you’ll need to plug it into a **power source** before using it.

#### Via Hard Drive Enclosure

**Hard drive enclosures** or **disk caddies** are cases that house and power a hard drive. It contains the necessary interface and components to be connected to a computer. Enclosures can also be connected **via USB** and are widely available in most computer stores.

#### Via a Docking Station

Another method to connect your laptop’s hard drive to a PC is through a **docking station**. It’s an **upgraded version** of a drive enclosure and can house up to **two hard drives**. However, you’ll need to make sure that the docking station’s drive bays are **compatible** with your hard drive.
