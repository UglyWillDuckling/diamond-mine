" **Mustang Sally** " is a [rhythm and blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues") (R&B) song written and first recorded by [Mack Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mack_Rice "Mack Rice") in [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_in_music "1965 in music").[^1] It was released on the Blue Rock label (4014) in May 1965 with "Sir Mack Rice" as the artist.[^2] The song uses an AAB layout with a 24-bar structure.[^3]

It gained greater popularity when [Wilson Pickett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson_Pickett "Wilson Pickett") [covered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_version "Cover version") it the following year on a single, a version that was also released on the 1966 album *[The Wicked Pickett](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Wicked_Pickett&action=edit&redlink=1 "The Wicked Pickett (page does not exist)")*.[^4] Also in 1966, [John Lee Hooker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lee_Hooker "John Lee Hooker") recorded an entirely different song with a similar title — "Mustang Sally & GTO."

In 2000, the [Wilson Pickett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson_Pickett "Wilson Pickett") version of "Mustang Sally" on [Atlantic Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Records "Atlantic Records") was inducted into the [Grammy Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Hall_of_Fame "Grammy Hall of Fame").[^5]

Rice was visiting singer [Della Reese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Della_Reese "Della Reese"), who was considering buying a new [Lincoln Continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Continental "Lincoln Continental") for her drummer and band leader Calvin Shields for his birthday. Rice and other band members were teasing Shields about the pending gift, and Shields replied that he did not want a Lincoln; he wanted a [Ford Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang "Ford Mustang"). Rice had never heard of the Mustang, which had just come out, but he teased Shields about wanting a smaller car. He decided there might be a song in the situation, changing it to be about a woman who doesn't want to do anything but ride around in her new car.[^7] Rice called the early version "Mustang Mama," but changed the title after [Aretha Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aretha_Franklin "Aretha Franklin") suggested "Mustang Sally" because he used the name Sally in the chorus.[^8]

Rice got part of the chorus from the children's game song (recorded by various artists) "Little Sally Walker," versions of which include the lyrics "Ride Sally ride, wipe your weepin' eyes," with variations.[^7] His variation goes, "All you wanna do is ride around, Sally/Ride, Sally, ride/One of these early mornings/You're gonna be wipin' your weepin' eyes."

In the liner notes for *The Rascals Anthology*, [Felix Cavaliere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Cavaliere "Felix Cavaliere") states that [The Young Rascals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Young_Rascals "The Young Rascals") recorded "Mustang Sally" and " [Land of a Thousand Dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_a_Thousand_Dances "Land of a Thousand Dances") " before Pickett and that [Atlantic Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Records "Atlantic Records") "copped those two songs from them and gave them to Pickett" to record. When Cavaliere does his flashback concerts, he also recounts how Rice thanked him for having been the [B-side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-side "B-side") of the Young Rascals' hit " [Good Lovin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Lovin%27 "Good Lovin'")," explaining that the royalties were paid by records sold — thus, the B-side writer was paid for an equal number of sales as the A-side.

Rice's version made it to No. 15 on the U.S. [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues") charts in 1965. Pickett's version climbed to No. 6 on the R&B charts and No. 23 on the [Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_music "Popular music") charts in 1966, No. 4 in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada") on the ([RPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPM_\(magazine\) "RPM (magazine)")) charts, and No. 28 [^9] in the [UK Singles Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart "UK Singles Chart") on its original release and No. 62, when it was released again in 1987.[^10]

In 2004, *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* ranked [Wilson Pickett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson_Pickett "Wilson Pickett") 's recording of the song at No. 434 on a list of [*Rolling Stone* ' s 500 Greatest Songs of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_500_Greatest_Songs_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Songs of All Time").[^11] The song dropped seven spots to No. 441, when the magazine published its 2010 update of the list.[^12]

- [Buddy Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Guy "Buddy Guy") recorded the song in 1991, featuring [Jeff Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Beck "Jeff Beck").[^13]
- The song featured prominently in the 1991 film *[The Commitments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Commitments_\(film\) "The Commitments (film)")* and appears on the film's soundtrack album, sung by [Andrew Strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Strong "Andrew Strong").[^14] It was released as a single from the album and reached No. 63 in the UK Singles Chart, No. 43 on the Australian charts and No. 17 on the New Zealand charts.[^15] In 2022 it was certified Silver by the [British Phonographic Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Phonographic_Industry "British Phonographic Industry") (BPI).[^16]
- When astronaut [Sally Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Ride "Sally Ride") became the first American woman in space on the [Space Shuttle *Challenger*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger "Space Shuttle Challenger") in 1983, many in the crowd attending the launch wore T-shirts printed with a play on the lyric, "Ride, Sally Ride." [^17]
- On [Fox's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company "Fox Broadcasting Company") TV show [*Glee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glee_\(TV_series\) "Glee (TV series)"), Noah Guthrie covered the song in the second episode of the sixth season (["Homecoming"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homecoming_\(Glee\) "Homecoming (Glee)")).
