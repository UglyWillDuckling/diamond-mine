If the cabinets above the counter have a flat underside, that's the best place for light, and you can do it without drilling.

Best options

**Rechargeable** LED bars with magnets or adhesive. 

They stick under the cabinet, often have a motion sensor, and you charge them by USB every few weeks. There are no cables at all, which suits an old kitchen. 

Plug-in LED strip in an aluminium channel. 

A strip with a diffuser channel gives even light without bright dots reflecting off the counter. Fix it with strong double-sided tape and run the cable to the nearest socket. Clean the surface with isopropyl alcohol first, because old cabinets are often greasy and the tape won't hold. Stick-on puck lights. These are battery-powered and cheap. They give spottier light, but they're good if you only need a couple. 

### If there are no cabinets above the counter

Clamp lamp on a shelf edge or the back of the counter, with a flexible neck so you can aim it where you cut. Small task lamp on the counter itself. It's the simplest option, though it takes up space. Magnetic lamp if there's any metal nearby, such as a range hood or a metal shelf. 

### Things that make a difference

Choose 3000-4000K (warm to neutral white) and CRI 90+ so food looks natural. Mount lights near the front edge of the cabinet underside, not against the wall. That puts light on your work area instead of behind your hands. If the counter is glossy, use diffused light to avoid glare.
