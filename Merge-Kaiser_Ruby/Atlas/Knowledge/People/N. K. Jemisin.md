**Nora Keita Jemisin** [^1] (born September 19, 1972) is an American [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction") and [fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy "Fantasy") writer. Her fiction includes a wide range of themes, notably cultural conflict and oppression.[^2] [^3] Her [debut novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debut_novel "Debut novel"), *[The Hundred Thousand Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hundred_Thousand_Kingdoms "The Hundred Thousand Kingdoms")* (2010), and the subsequent books in her *[Inheritance Trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Inheritance_Trilogy_\(Jemisin_series\) "The Inheritance Trilogy (Jemisin series)")* received critical acclaim.

Jemisin has won several awards for her work, including multiple [Locus Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locus_Award "Locus Award"), starting with her [first novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locus_Award_for_Best_First_Novel "Locus Award for Best First Novel"). Her [*Broken Earth* series](#Broken_Earth_series) made her the first African-American author to win the [Hugo Award for Best Novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Award_for_Best_Novel "Hugo Award for Best Novel"), as well as the first author to win in three consecutive years, and the first to win for all three novels in a trilogy.[^4] She won a fourth Hugo in 2020 for the novelette *[Emergency Skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Skin "Emergency Skin")* and a fifth in 2022 for the comic book series *[Far Sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Sector "Far Sector")*.[^5] [^6] Jemisin was a recipient of the [MacArthur Fellows Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program "MacArthur Fellows Program") prize in 2020.[^7]

In 2025, the [Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Fiction_and_Fantasy_Writers_Association "Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers Association") named Jemisin the 42nd Damon Knight Memorial [Grand Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SFWA_Grand_Master "SFWA Grand Master") in recognition of her significant contributions to the literature of science fiction and fantasy.[^8]

## Early life

Jemisin was born in [Iowa City, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_City,_Iowa "Iowa City, Iowa"), while her parents Noah Jemisin and Janice Jemisin were completing masters programs at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa "University of Iowa").[^9] She grew up in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") and [Mobile, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile,_Alabama "Mobile, Alabama"). Jemisin attended [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University "Tulane University") from 1990 to 1994, where she received a B.S. in [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology"). She went on to study counseling and earn her [Master of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Education "Master of Education") from the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park "University of Maryland, College Park"). She lived in Massachusetts for ten years and then moved to New York City.[^10] She worked as a mental health and career counselor before writing full-time.[^10] [^2]

## Career

A graduate of the 2002 [Viable Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viable_Paradise "Viable Paradise") writing workshop,[^11] [^12] Jemisin has published short stories and novels. She was a member of the Boston-area writing group BRAWLers,[^13] and as of 2010 was a member of Altered Fluid, a speculative fiction critique group.[^13] In 2009 and 2010, Jemisin's short story " [Non-Zero Probabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Zero_Probabilities "Non-Zero Probabilities") " was a finalist for the [Nebula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebula_Award_for_Best_Short_Story "Nebula Award for Best Short Story") and [Hugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Award_for_Best_Short_Story "Hugo Award for Best Short Story") Best Short Story Awards.[^14]

Jemisin's [debut novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debut_novel "Debut novel"), *[The Hundred Thousand Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hundred_Thousand_Kingdoms "The Hundred Thousand Kingdoms")*, the first volume in her *Inheritance Trilogy*, was published in 2010. It was a finalist for the 2010 [Nebula Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebula_Award "Nebula Award") [^15] and shortlisted for the [James Tiptree Jr. Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otherwise_Award "Otherwise Award") (now called the Otherwise Award).[^16] In 2011, it was a finalist for the [Hugo Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Award "Hugo Award"),[^17] [World Fantasy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Fantasy_Award "World Fantasy Award"),[^18] and [Locus Award for Best First Novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locus_Award_for_Best_First_Novel "Locus Award for Best First Novel"), winning the latter.[^19] It was followed by two further novels in the same trilogy – *[The Broken Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Broken_Kingdoms "The Broken Kingdoms")* (2010) and *[The Kingdom of Gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kingdom_of_Gods "The Kingdom of Gods")* (2011).

NK Jemisin in 2011

During her delivery of the Guest of Honour speech at the 2013 Continuum in Australia, Jemisin pointed out that 10% of the [Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Fiction_and_Fantasy_Writers_Association "Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers Association") (SFWA) membership voted for [alt-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt-right "Alt-right") writer Theodore Beale, known as [Vox Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vox_Day "Vox Day"), in his bid for the SFWA presidential position, stating that silence about Beale's views was the same as enabling them.[^20] Canadian writer [Amal El-Mohtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amal_El-Mohtar "Amal El-Mohtar") characterized Beale's response to Jemisin as "an appallingly racist screed".[^21] A link to his comments was tweeted on the SFWA Authors [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") feed, and Beale was subsequently expelled from the organization after a unanimous vote by the SFWA Board.[^22]

Jemisin was a co- [Guest of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction_convention#Guests_of_Honor "Science fiction convention") of the 2014 [WisCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiscon "Wiscon") [science fiction convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction_convention "Science fiction convention") in [Madison, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin "Madison, Wisconsin").[^23] At that time, *[GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ "GQ")* described her as having "a day job as a counseling psychologist." [^24] She was the Author Guest of Honor at [Arisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arisia "Arisia") 2015 in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston"), Massachusetts.[^25] In January 2016, Jemisin started writing "Otherworldly", a bimonthly column for *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*.[^26] In May 2016, Jemisin mounted a [Patreon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patreon "Patreon") campaign which raised sufficient funding to allow her to quit her job as a counselor and focus full-time on her writing.[^27]

### Broken Earth

Jemisin's novel *[The Fifth Season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fifth_Season_\(novel\) "The Fifth Season (novel)")* was published in 2015, the first of the *Broken Earth* trilogy. The novel was inspired in part from a dream Jemisin had and the protests in [Ferguson, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferguson,_Missouri "Ferguson, Missouri") about the death of [Michael Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferguson_unrest "Ferguson unrest").[^28] [^29] *The Fifth Season* won the [Hugo Award for Best Novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Award_for_Best_Novel "Hugo Award for Best Novel"), making Jemisin the first [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans") writer to win a Hugo award in that category.[^30] The sequels in the trilogy, *[The Obelisk Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Obelisk_Gate "The Obelisk Gate")* and *[The Stone Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stone_Sky "The Stone Sky")*, won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 2017 [^31] and 2018,[^32] respectively, making Jemisin the first author to win the [Hugo Award for Best Novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Award_for_Best_Novel "Hugo Award for Best Novel") in three consecutive years, as well as the first to win for all three novels in a trilogy.[^4] In 2017, [*Bustle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bustle_\(magazine\) "Bustle (magazine)") called Jemisin "the sci-fi writer every woman needs to be reading".[^33]

With Mac Walters, Jemisin co-authored the 2017 book *[Mass Effect: Andromeda Initiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_Effect:_Andromeda_\(book_series\)#Initiation "Mass Effect: Andromeda (book series)")*, the second in a book series based on the video game *[Mass Effect: Andromeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_Effect:_Andromeda "Mass Effect: Andromeda")*.[^34] Jemisin published a short story collection, *[How Long 'til Black Future Month?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_Long_%27til_Black_Future_Month%3F "How Long 'til Black Future Month?")* in November 2018.[^35] It contains stories written from 2004 to 2017 and four new works. *[Far Sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Sector "Far Sector")*, a twelve-issue limited series comic written by Jemisin with art by Jamal Campbell, began publication in 2019. It was nominated for the 2021 [Eisner Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisner_Awards "Eisner Awards") for Best Limited Series.[^36]

Jemisin's urban fantasy novel *[The City We Became](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_City_We_Became "The City We Became")* was published in March 2020. In October 2020, Jemisin was announced as a recipient of the [MacArthur Fellows Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program "MacArthur Fellows Program") Genius Grant.[^7] In June 2021, Sony's [TriStar Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TriStar_Pictures "TriStar Pictures") won the rights to adapt *The Broken Earth* trilogy in a seven-figure deal with Jemisin adapting the novels for the screen herself.[^37] In 2021, she was included in the [*Time* 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100"), *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\) "Time (magazine)")* 's annual list of the 100 most influential people in the world.[^38] *The World We Make*, a sequel to Jemisin's 2020 novel, was released in November 2022.

## Personal life

Jemisin lives and works in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), New York.[^39] She is first cousin once removed to stand-up comic and television host [W. Kamau Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._Kamau_Bell "W. Kamau Bell").[^40] [^41]
