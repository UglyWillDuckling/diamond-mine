**Systems thinking** is a way of making sense of the complexity of the world by looking at it [in terms of wholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holism "Holism") and [relationships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relationalism "Relationalism") rather than by splitting it down into its parts.[^5] [^6] It has been used as a way of exploring and developing effective action in complex contexts,[^7] enabling [systems change](#Applications).[^8] [^9] Systems thinking draws on and contributes to [systems theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_theory "Systems theory") and the [system sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_science "Systems science").[^10]

## History

### Ptolemaic system versus the Copernican system

The term *[system](#System)* is [polysemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysemy "Polysemy"): [Robert Hooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hooke "Robert Hooke") (1674) used it in multiple senses, in his System of the World,[^11]<sup><span title="Page / location: p.24">: p.24</span> </sup> but also in the sense of the [Ptolemaic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_system "Ptolemaic system") versus the [Copernican system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernican_heliocentrism "Copernican heliocentrism") [^12]<sup><span title="Page / location: 450">: 450</span> </sup> of the relation of the planets to the fixed stars [^13] which are cataloged in [Hipparchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hipparchus_star_catalog "Hipparchus star catalog") ' and [Ptolemy's Star catalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almagest "Almagest").[^14] Hooke's claim was answered in magisterial detail by Newton's (1687) *[Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophi%C3%A6_Naturalis_Principia_Mathematica "Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica")*, Book three, *The System of the World* [^15]<sup><span title="Page / location: Book three">: Book three</span> </sup> (that is, *the system of the world* is a [physical system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_system "Physical system")).[^11]

Newton's approach, using [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_system "Dynamical system") continues to this day.[^12] In brief, Newton's equations (a [system of equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_of_equations "System of equations")) have methods for [their solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equation_solving "Equation solving").

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Ideal_feedback_model.svg/500px-Ideal_feedback_model.svg.png)

System output can be controlled with feedback.

By 1824, the [Carnot cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnot_cycle "Carnot cycle") presented an engineering challenge, which was how to maintain the operating temperatures of the hot and cold working fluids of the [physical plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_plant "Physical plant").[^16] In 1868, [James Clerk Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell "James Clerk Maxwell") presented a framework for, and a limited solution to, the problem of controlling the rotational speed of a physical plant.[^17] Maxwell's solution echoed [James Watt's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Watt "James Watt") [(1784) centrifugal moderator (denoted as element **Q**)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SteamEngine_Boulton%26Watt_1784.png "File:SteamEngine Boulton&Watt 1784.png") for maintaining (but not enforcing) the constant speed of a physical plant (that is, *Q* represents a moderator, but not a governor, by Maxwell's definition).[^18] [^1]

Maxwell's approach, which linearized the equations of motion of the system, produced a tractable method of solution.[^18]<sup><span title="Page / location: 428–429">: 428–429</span> </sup> [Norbert Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener "Norbert Wiener") identified this approach as an influence on his studies of [cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybernetics "Cybernetics") [^2] during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") [^18] and Wiener even proposed treating some [subsystems](#subsystem) under investigation as [black boxes](#black_box).[^22]<sup><span title="Page / location: 242">: 242</span> </sup> Methods for solutions of the systems of equations then become the subject of study, as in [feedback control systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedback_control_system "Feedback control system"), in [stability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stability_theory "Stability theory"), in [constraint satisfaction problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constraint_satisfaction_problem "Constraint satisfaction problem"), the [unification algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_algorithm "Unification algorithm"), [type inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindley%E2%80%93Milner_type_system "Hindley–Milner type system"), and so forth.

Systems thinking, born from the visionary contributions of theoretical biologist [Ludwig von Bertalanffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_von_Bertalanffy "Ludwig von Bertalanffy"), computer scientist [Jay Forrester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Forrester "Jay Forrester"), and their contemporaries, reached its zenith in the 1990s with the release of [Peter Senge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Senge "Peter Senge") ’s seminal work, [The Fifth Discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fifth_Discipline "The Fifth Discipline"), a landmark in intellectual exploration.[^23]

> ...What is a system? A system is a set of things... interconnected in such a way that they produce their own pattern of behavior over time.... But the system’s response to these forces is characteristic of itself, and that response is seldom simple in the real world

— Donella Meadows [^24]<sup><span title="Page / location: 2">: 2</span></sup>

> \[a system\] is "an integrated whole even though composed of diverse, interacting, specialized structures and subjunctions"

— IEEE (1972) [^21]<sup><span title="Page / location: 582">: 582</span></sup>

"a system is a collection of things that are interconnected and interdependent from which stuff emerges" [^34]

\-Walls & Flach (2025) [^34]

- [Subsystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System#Subsystems "System") serve as part of a larger system, but each comprises a system in its own right. Each frequently can be described reductively, with properties obeying its own laws, such as Newton's System of the World, in which entire [planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet "Planet"), [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star "Star"), and their satellites can be treated, sometimes in a scientific way as dynamical systems, entirely mathematically, as demonstrated by [Johannes Kepler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kepler "Johannes Kepler") 's equation (1619) for the orbit of Mars before Newton's *Principia* appeared in 1687.
- [Black boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box "Black box") are subsystems whose operation can be characterized by their inputs and outputs, [without regard to further detail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viable_system_model#Regulatory_aphorisms "Viable system model").[^24]<sup><span title="Page / location: 87–88">: 87–88</span> </sup> [^35]

### Particular systems

- [Political systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_system "Political system") were recognized as early as the millennia before the common era.[^36] [^37]
- [Biological systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_system "Biological system") were recognized in [Aristotle's lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalloni#Bay_and_lagoon "Kalloni") ca. 350 BCE.[^38] [^39]
- [Economic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_system "Economic system") were recognized by 1776.[^40]
- [Social systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_system "Social system") were recognized by the 19th and 20th centuries of the common era.[^41] [^42]
- [Radar systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_system "Radar system") were developed in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory#Formation "MIT Radiation Laboratory") in subsystem fashion; they were made up of [transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmitter "Transmitter"), [receiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_warning_receiver "Radar warning receiver"), [power supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_supply "Power supply"), and [signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_processing "Signal processing") subsystems, to defend against airborne attacks.[^43]
- [Dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems "Dynamical systems") of [ordinary differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equation "Ordinary differential equation") were shown to exhibit [stable behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stability_theory "Stability theory") given a [suitable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control-Lyapunov_function "Control-Lyapunov function") [Lyapunov control function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyapunov_function#Example "Lyapunov function") by [Aleksandr Lyapunov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Lyapunov "Aleksandr Lyapunov") in 1892.[^44]
- [Thermodynamic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_systems "Thermodynamic systems") were treated as early as the eighteenth century, in which it was discovered that [heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat "Heat") could be created without limit, but that for [closed systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_system "Closed system"), [laws of thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_thermodynamics "Laws of thermodynamics") could be formulated.[^45] [Ilya Prigogine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Prigogine "Ilya Prigogine") (1980) has identified situations in which systems far from equilibrium can exhibit stable behavior;[^46] once a Lyapunov function has been identified, future and past can be distinguished, and scientific activity can begin.[^45]<sup><span title="Page / location: 212–213">: 212–213</span></sup>

### Systems far from equilibrium

Living systems are [resilient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_resilience "Ecological resilience"),[^29] and are [far from equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-equilibrium_thermodynamics "Non-equilibrium thermodynamics").[^24]<sup><span title="Page / location: Ch.3">: Ch.3</span> </sup> [^46] [Homeostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostasis "Homeostasis") is the analog to equilibrium, for a living system; the concept was described in 1849, and the term was coined in 1926.[^47] [^48]

Resilient systems are [self-organizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organization "Self-organization");[^29] [^4] [^24]<sup><span title="Page / location: Ch.3">: Ch.3</span> </sup> [^49]

The scope of functional controls is [hierarchical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy "Hierarchy"), in a resilient system.[^29] [^24]<sup><span title="Page / location: Ch.3">: Ch.3</span></sup>

## Frameworks and methodologies

Frameworks and methodologies for systems thinking include:

- Critical systems heuristics:[^50] in particular, there can be twelve boundary categories for the systems when organizing one's thinking and actions.
- [Critical systems thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_systems_thinking "Critical systems thinking"), including the [E P I C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_systems_thinking#Recent_developments "Critical systems thinking") approach.
- [DSRP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSRP "DSRP"), a framework for systems thinking that attempts to generalise all other approaches.
- [Ontology engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_engineering "Ontology engineering") of representation, formal naming and definition of categories, and the properties and the relations between concepts, data, and entities.
- [Soft systems methodology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_systems_methodology "Soft systems methodology"), including the [CATWOE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_systems_methodology#CATWOE "Soft systems methodology") approach and [rich pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_pictures "Rich pictures").
- [Systemic design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systemic_design "Systemic design"), for example using the [double diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Diamond_\(design_process_model\) "Double Diamond (design process model)") approach.
- [System dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_dynamics "System dynamics") of stocks, flows, and internal [feedback loops](#Feedback_control_systems).
- [Viable system model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viable_system_model "Viable system model"): uses 5 subsystems.
