Almost seven long years ago, the intrepid staff of IGN PS2 decided to cobble together some of the highlights of the PlayStation 2's then-booming game library. Today, we're updating it with a few titles they hadn't even dreamed of back in the day.Plenty has changed since this article originally ran: the PS2 is no longer the king of the hill, and things are finally starting to wind down after a decade of absolutely killer games. With the passage of time, however, the landscape of multiplayer gaming -- co-op in particular -- has shifted too, with online becoming the preferred way to game with friends rather than sharing a couch.  
  
As such, we've decided to dive back into this feature and the PS2's library to see if we could find a few more gems that make playing with friends a can't-miss experience. Sure, these games may feel a little limited in what they do compared to the always-on, shared ID experiences available on today's console, but then the PS2's online service wasn't exactly known for being cutting-edge even in its heyday. So join us, won't you? Take a walk down memory lane as we add a bit of minty freshness to an already great list. [Champions of Norrath: Realms of EverQuest](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/568/568803.html)  
- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6kso?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games) / [See it on Amazon (UK)](https://r.zdbb.net/u/8r78?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

When looking for games that have hit since our original feature was whipped up, almost instantly [Snowblind Studios](https://games.ign.com/objects/026/026626.html) ' take on Sony's own EverQuest world came to mind. Sure, we've already got their Baldur's Gate dungeon crawler on our list, but Champions of Norrath is the culmination of those original concepts. Plus, it sports not only local co-op, but online, too, and some of the prettiest graphics you're ever likely to see pumping out of a standard def TV.  
  
The key was using a technique called supersampling. Because everything in Snowblind's engine was tile-based (meaning they could use little bits and pieces of high-res artwork rather than trying to model everything wholesale from polygons), the worlds could not only deliver the kind of detail that you'd normally equate with more advanced Xbox techniques like bump mapping, but everything was rendered at a resolution *higher* than the normal PS2 output and then scaled down. The result? Well, throw the game in and see for yourself. You'll be impressed.  
  

Nothing beat stompin' monsters with friends.

  
  
[Buzz! The Mega Quiz](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/883/883082.html)  
- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6ksp?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games) / [See it on Amazon (UK)](https://r.zdbb.net/u/8r79?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

It's probably a stretch to call Buzz a "cooperative" experience because you're basically playing head-to-head with everyone else, but if we've noticed anything about Sony's little quiz show, it's that alliances end up forming -- and then they're summarily destroyed when it comes to the final round. In the end, though, even when you're playing against a bunch of friends, you're still playing *with* them, and who doesn't love a friendly little drinking game (apple juice, of course) mixed in with their quiz show?  
  
Buzz owes plenty to its addictive pull, but nothing is quite so powerful as the sort of on-the-fly wheeling and dealing that can come from a handful of players banding together to take down someone who, say, just happens to be plain more knowledgeable than they are. In Buzz!, knowledge isn't necessarily power, but tag-teaming nearly always is.

[SingStar 90s](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/946/946937.html)  
- [See it on Amazon (UK)](https://r.zdbb.net/u/8r7a?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

Simultaneously the greatest and most painful thing you're ever likely to hear is two friends attempting to harmonize to songs they think they know. Sure, it's a train wreck -- and when you end up taking up the mic with someone for a duet you'll probably end up crashing and burning spectacularly, too -- but that's part of the fun. For those that can actually sing, there's always cranking the game's judging difficulty up to Hard and belting it out for points, but for most of us, it's about trying to find the right pitch to a harmony you didn't know you didn't actually know until the song starts, and it's a blast.  
  
Of course, there will inevitably be two shrinking violets who suddenly turn into whatever diva you prefer to imagine when you stick a SingStar mic in their hands. They'll belt out choruses with enough power to impress Freddie Mercury from the great beyond and shame everyone else even through the liquid courage. This may be the beginning of the end for nearly everyone else, but it's usually the *start* of the night for the secret sirens.

Where would music games be without [Harmonix](https://games.ign.com/objects/027/027660.html)? Apparently, still relegated to "just" singing or "just" playing our clicky plastic guitars, which is no way to get together with friends and rock out all-inclusively. Though the original Rock Band brought two guitars, drums and vocals together for the first time in one game, the sequel has emerged as a much more long-term offering thanks to plenty of challenges and modes that were missing from the original PS2 release. No, you can't play online (or, uh, do *anything* online, downloading songs included), but at least you can rock out with your friends in the same living room.  
  
Being able to do so completely changed the idea of what a rhythm action game was. Instead of just following along to the stuff on screen or even indulging your inner rock star, you were now a group, and *everyone* could play make-believe together. If that sounds strange, you clearly haven't experienced the full band rush that is Rock Band in action. Clearly this must be remedied post-haste.

[Guitar Hero: Metallica](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/142/14294149.html)  
- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6kss?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games) / [See it on Amazon (UK)](https://r.zdbb.net/u/8r7g?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

It would be impossible to mention Rock Band without mentioning the series that started it all: Guitar Hero. When Harmonix left to do their own thing and Neversoft picked up the slack, some felt the series didn't really progress -- not until Metallica added its own special sauce to the mix. Not only is GH: Metallica a loving tribute to the band filled with factoids and bonus footage, it's a great collection of songs supporting guitars, vocals and drums with the all-important inclusion of the almighty double bass and resulting Expert+ difficulty to the mix.  
  
The inclusion of Metallica, obviously, wasn't just a name slapped on a familiar engine; instead, it becomes something of a portal into the world of a truly great band with a surprising amount of variety to the tracks. In as much as Rock Band let you become just about anyone (including your own band), this game lets you be Metallica -- if only for a few hours. Ah, but what wonderful hours they are.

*Original Picks*  
  
Before [Sony Computer Entertainment](https://games.ign.com/objects/025/025653.html) America began its online effort last summer, PS2 fans of multiplayer games could entertain themselves with what used to be quite standard fare: A two-player versus mode. Maybe, if a dev team's coders were animal-masochists they'd add in the four-player deathmatch mode using the Multi-tap, but anyone interested in multi-player goodness was generally out of luck. And frankly, one-on-one deathmatch isn't all that fun. So? Gamers either had to trod back to the dying arcades or buck up and upgrade their heaving old PCs.  
  
In the last few years publishers and developers alike have learned that Cooperative modes are quite popular. In response, they've slowly begun to add co-op modes into their games. The co-op game, an idea surely originated in the arcades back in the '80s with games like *Ikari Warriors*, *Contra* and *Gauntlet*, is an excellent alternative to the dull one-on-one deathmatch, and for those who don't have a Multi-tap, or who can't find one, can team up with their buddies in these excellent co-op additions.  
  
The PlayStation 2 isn't teeming with co-op games, but there are dozens of them, really good games in fact, waiting you and your friends to get to grips with. For the purposes of this article, we have singled out the list of co-op games that genuinely enable players to plat co-operatively with another. We're not focusing on four-player deathmatch games or online games per say, but games that provide two or more players to team up and play.  
  
**Full Game Co-op**  
  
Full Game Co-op titles are games providing players with the ability to play through the entire title from beginning to end in co-op mode.  
  
[**Twisted Metal: Black**](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/015/015456.html)  
- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6kst?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

  
  
Incog's first next generation attempt was one of those rare gems that began to usher in the spectacular year of 2001. The single-player game, filled with spectacular interactive backgrounds, creepy storylines, and constructed with a beautiful menu system, is also a four-player deathmatch game, but it also enables two players to team up and rifle through the game as a single force. And, yes, it's damn dark fun.  
  
[**Dynasty Warriors 4**](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/497/497746.html)  
- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6ksu?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

  
  
KOEI's brilliantly textured game may have some rough edges in its powerful engine, but with each new successive title in the series (there are three in this iteration of the DW series), it's been refined, bolstered and given gamers a little bit more. *[Dynasty Warriors 3](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/016/016328.html)* added two-player co-op mode, and *DW4* made it better. Run around, slash players, strategize to attack big groups and show off to one another for the biggest combos.

- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6ksv?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

Players appear on screen and skillfully avoid each other as they plow through dozens of enemies in three major areas.  
  
[**Baldur's Gate: Dark Alliance**](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/015/015580.html)  
- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6ksw?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

  
  
Now here's a game that has a wonderful singe-player quest; it's got a rich storyline, intuitive gameplay mechanics, and some of the most subtle, yet impressive graphics around. And as great as the game is by oneself, the journey is significantly more fun with a friend. From a pure combat standpoint, *[Baldur's Gate: Dark Alliance](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/015/015580.html)* is a hack-n-slash game not unlike another game on our list, *[Gauntlet: Dark Legacy](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/014/014940.html)* (what's up with all this "dark" anyways?). But where *Baldur's Gate* surpasses the Atari classic is with its story, graphics and depth. Additionally, we receive a TON of emails from female gamers that love the game, so maybe all you male gamers can use it to find your long-lost soul mate. Easily the best PS2 game in the genre, *Baldur's Gate: Dark Alliance* will have you pledging to it every morning at 7 am sharp. Play it.

[**Gauntlet Legends: Dark Legacy**](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/014/014940.html)  
  
- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6ksx?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

  
  
Like *Contra*, *Gauntlet* is yet another classic game that is well remembered for its multiplayer mayhem. But *unlike* *Contra*, *Gauntlet* upped the ante by implementing a four-player co-op mode, rather than just two. The addition of two more players was significant, and it alone made a somewhat dismal single-player, or even two-player game, markedly better. Just know this: Smashing and breaking things is fun, but with three other friends, it's like vanilla ice cream topped with Gummi Bears on a hot summer day. 'Nuff said.  
  
[**Contra: Shattered Soldier**](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/482/482096.html)  
  
- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6ksy?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

  
  
Contra is a game franchise that needs no introduction. From its infamous thirty-man code to the awesome two-player co-op it brought to the NES, *Contra* games have been a mainstay on just about every console since its inception, and that includes the PS2.  
  
*[Contra: Shattered Soldier](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/482/482096.html)* brings back the classic 2D gameplay of the original, ups the graphics tenfold, and gives new meaning to the phrase "ass-kicking hard." But more important still, it retains the signature two-player co-op found in just about every *Contra* game in existence, and that's is what's important here. *Contra: SS* is, without a doubt, one of the best co-op games on the PS2; it looks amazing, plays great, and there is no better way to let out some built-up frustration (though the game's difficulty itself may drive you up a wall).  
  
[**TimeSplitters 2**](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/016/016581.html)  
- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6kt0?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

  
  
In addition to a great, fast-paced four-player mode, Free Radical's first-person shooter brings one of the best co-op modes out there to the table. Two players can play through the entire game together, from the *GoldenEye* -inspired dam all the way to the final battle. The cooperative mode is brilliantly balanced, neither dumbed down nor sloppily made more difficult. The goals remain the same, the objectives identical, but players must use their wits to ration supplies between the team -- if there is only one sniper rifle on the ground and one of the players picks it up, another one does not magically appear in the second gamer's arsenal; rather, he or she simply goes without it. One of the best.

- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6kt1?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

  
  
In the most classic way to stretch the life of the game's potential, [Activision](https://games.ign.com/objects/025/025004.html) and K2 ditched the create-a-level mode from the past game and added a versus and co-op mode. The Versus is great fun, especially when players open up all of the extra characters, and the Co-op is just as keen, but it requires that players genuinely play together to sneak through a handful of missions and levels. True co-op play if we've ever seen it.  
  
[**Half-Life**](https://ps2.ign.com/objects/015/015631.html)  
- [See it on Amazon](https://r.zdbb.net/u/6kt4?or=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ign.com%2Farticles%2F2010%2F05%2F07%2Fthe-best-ps2-co-op-games)

  
  
Gearbox's excellent port, or re-creation, of Valve's infamous first-person shooter not only looks better on PS2, it offers three modes, Story, Head-to-Head and Decay, which is the co-op mode. Decay takes place during Gordon's adventure in *Half-Life*, and enables players to select an AI bot with which they can switch back and forth during the game (in full screen), or with another human character (via split-screen). It's good fun for Half-Life maniacs but it requires a certain level of coordination that requires much yelling and cursing at one another -- all part of the social interaction that makes co-op games so lovely.

**Sports Games**  

  
  
Any sports game worth its salt has at least a one-on-one mode, and the newest and best offer at least a two-on-two mode. Football games offer even more. Sports games are all designed to play against the AI, but the two-, four-, six- or eight-player modes enhance and deepen the experience of any sports game. From *Madden NFL 2003* to *NFL 2K3* to *NBA Live*, *Sega Sports Tennis* or EA's latest hoops games, *NBA Street Vol. 2*, there are two-on-two modes worth dozens of hours of play. We didn't list them all because, well heck, they all have co-op modes build right in!  
  
In the end, Co-op games are a major part of the gaming social lexicon. They ask us to work together, to play together and to think not only about the challenges of the particular game, but of the people we're playing with. Should one be more aggressive or more of a gatherer? Should one hog all the power-ups or show compassion to their teammate? Whatever the case may be, co-op games show off the best aspects of socializing in gaming, and we believe there should be more of them, offline or online.
