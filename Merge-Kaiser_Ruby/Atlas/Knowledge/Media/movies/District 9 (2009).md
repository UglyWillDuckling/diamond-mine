***District 9*** is a 2009 [science fiction action film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction_action_film) directed by [Neill Blomkamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neill_Blomkamp) in his feature film debut, written by Blomkamp and [Terri Tatchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terri_Tatchell), and produced by [Peter Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Jackson) and [Carolynne Cunningham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolynne_Cunningham). It is a co-production of [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand), the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), and [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa). The film stars [Sharlto Copley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharlto_Copley), [Jason Cope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Cope), and [David James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_James_(actor,_born_1972)), and was adapted from Blomkamp's 2006 short film *Alive in Joburg*.

The film is partially presented in a [found footage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Found_footage_(film_technique)) format by featuring fictional interviews, news footage, and video from surveillance cameras. The story, which explores themes of [humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanity_(virtue)), [xenophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophobia) and [social segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_segregation), begins in an [alternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternate_history) 1982, when an alien spaceship appears over [Johannesburg, South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg,_South_Africa). When a population of sick and malnourished insectoid aliens is discovered on the ship, the South African government confines them to an internment camp called District 9. Twenty years later, during the government's relocation of the aliens to another camp, one of the confined aliens named Christopher Johnson, who is about to try to escape from Earth with his son and return home, crosses paths with a bureaucrat named Wikus van de Merwe leading the relocation. The title and premise of *District 9* were inspired by events in [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town)'s [District Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Six), during the [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_under_apartheid) era.

A [viral marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_marketing) campaign for the film began in 2008 at [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con), while the theatrical trailer debuted in July 2009. *District 9* was released by [TriStar Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TriStar_Pictures) on 14 August 2009, in North America and became a financial success, earning over $210 million at the box office. It also received acclaim from critics, with some calling it one of the best science fiction films of the 2000s, and garnered numerous awards and nominations, including four [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards) nominations for [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture), [Best Adapted Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Adapted_Screenplay), [Best Visual Effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Visual_Effects), and [Best Film Editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Film_Editing).[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_9#cite_note-7)
