**David Stephen Mitchell** (born 12 January 1969) is an English novelist, screenwriter, and translator.

He has written nine novels, two of which, *[number9dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number9dream "Number9dream")* (2001) and *[Cloud Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_Atlas_\(novel\) "Cloud Atlas (novel)")* (2004), were shortlisted for the [Booker Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_Prize "Booker Prize"). He has also written articles for several newspapers, most notably for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")*. He has translated books about [autism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autism "Autism") from Japanese to English.

He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Literature "Royal Society of Literature") in 2013.[^1]

## Early life

Mitchell was born in [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport "Southport") in Lancashire (now [Merseyside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merseyside "Merseyside")), England, and raised in [Malvern, Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvern,_Worcestershire "Malvern, Worcestershire"). He was educated at [Hanley Castle High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanley_Castle_High_School "Hanley Castle High School"). At the [University of Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kent "University of Kent"), he earned a degree in English and [American Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Literature "American Literature"), followed by an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in [Comparative Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_literature "Comparative literature").

Mitchell lived in [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily "Sicily") for a year. He moved to [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima "Hiroshima"), Japan, where he taught English to technical students for eight years, before returning to England. There he could live on his earnings as a writer and support his pregnant wife.[^2]

## Career

### Prose

Mitchell's first novel, *[Ghostwritten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghostwritten_\(novel\) "Ghostwritten (novel)")* (1999), takes place in locations ranging from [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa,_Japan "Okinawa, Japan") to [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia "Mongolia") to pre-millennial New York City, as nine narrators tell stories that interlock and intersect. It won the [John Llewellyn Rhys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Llewellyn_Rhys_Prize "John Llewellyn Rhys Prize") (for best work of British literature written by an author under 35) and was shortlisted for the [Guardian First Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_First_Book_Award "Guardian First Book Award").[^3] His two subsequent novels, *[number9dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number9dream "Number9dream")* (2001) and *[Cloud Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_Atlas_\(novel\) "Cloud Atlas (novel)")* (2004), were both favourably received and shortlisted for the [Man Booker Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Booker_Prize "Man Booker Prize").[^4]

In 2003, he was selected as one of [Granta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granta "Granta") 's Best of Young British Novelists.[^5] In 2007, Mitchell was listed among [*Time*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\) "Time (magazine)") magazine's 100 Most Influential People in The World.[^6]

In 2012, his [metafictional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metafiction "Metafiction") novel *[Cloud Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_Atlas_\(novel\) "Cloud Atlas (novel)")* (again, with multiple narrators), was adapted as [a feature film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_Atlas_\(film\) "Cloud Atlas (film)") of the same name.

One segment of *number9dream* was adapted as a short film titled *[The Voorman Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Voorman_Problem "The Voorman Problem")* and starring [Martin Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Freeman "Martin Freeman"). It was nominated for a BAFTA in 2013.[^7]

In addition to novels, Mitchell has written opera libretti in recent years. *Wake*, with music by [Klaas de Vries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaas_de_Vries_\(composer\) "Klaas de Vries (composer)"), was based on the 2000 [Enschede fireworks disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enschede_fireworks_disaster "Enschede fireworks disaster"). It was performed by the Dutch Nationale Reisopera in 2010.[^8] He created the opera, *Sunken Garden*, with Dutch composer [Michel van der Aa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_van_der_Aa "Michel van der Aa"); it was premiered in 2013 by the [English National Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_National_Opera "English National Opera").[^9]

Several of Mitchell's book covers were created by design duo Kai and Sunny.[^10] Mitchell has also collaborated with the duo, by contributing two short stories to their art exhibits in 2011 and 2014.

Mitchell's sixth novel, *[The Bone Clocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bone_Clocks "The Bone Clocks")*, was published in 2014.[^11] In an interview in *[The Spectator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spectator "The Spectator")*, Mitchell said that the novel has "dollops of the fantastic in it", and is about "stuff between life and death".[^12] *The Bone Clocks* was longlisted for the [2014 Man Booker Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Man_Booker_Prize "2014 Man Booker Prize") [^13] and won the [World Fantasy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Fantasy_Award%E2%80%94Novel "World Fantasy Award—Novel").[^14]

Mitchell was the second author to contribute to the [Future Library project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Library_project "Future Library project"). He delivered his book *[From Me Flows What You Call Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_Me_Flows_What_You_Call_Time_\(novella\) "From Me Flows What You Call Time (novella)")* on 28 May 2016.[^15] [^16]

*[Utopia Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopia_Avenue "Utopia Avenue")*, Mitchell's ninth novel, was published by Hodder & Stoughton in 2020, during the first year of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic").[^17] *Utopia Avenue* tells the "unexpurgated story" of a British band of the same name, who emerged from London's psychedelic scene in 1967 and was "fronted by folk singer Elf Holloway, guitar demigod Jasper de Zoet and blues bassist Dean Moss".[^18]

Mitchell's entire body of fictional works feature multiple recurring characters and themes that together form an interconnected fictional world, which Mitchell refers to as his 'macronovel'.[^19]

### Screenwriting

Following the release of [the 2012 film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_Atlas_\(film\) "Cloud Atlas (film)") of *Cloud Atlas*, Mitchell began work as a screenwriter with [Lana Wachowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lana_Wachowski "Lana Wachowski") (one of *Cloud Atlas* ' three directors). In 2015, Mitchell contributed plotting and scripted scenes for the second season of the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") series *[Sense8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sense8 "Sense8")* by [the Wachowskis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wachowskis "The Wachowskis"), writing the series finale together with [Aleksandar Hemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Hemon "Aleksandar Hemon").[^20] Mitchell had signed a contract to write season three of the series, but Netflix cancelled the show.[^21]

In August 2019, it was announced that Mitchell would continue his collaboration with Lana Wachowski and Hemon to write the screenplay for *[The Matrix Resurrections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Matrix_Resurrections "The Matrix Resurrections")*.[^22]

## Personal life

After another stint in Japan, Mitchell and his wife, Keiko Yoshida, live in [Ardfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardfield "Ardfield"), County Cork, Ireland, as of 2018. They have two children.[^23] In an essay for [Random House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_House "Random House"), Mitchell wrote:[^24]

> I knew I wanted to be a writer since I was a kid, but until I came to Japan to live in 1994 I was too easily distracted to do much about it. I would probably have become a writer wherever I lived, but would I have become the same writer if I'd spent the last six years in London, or [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town"), or [Moose Jaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moose_Jaw "Moose Jaw"), on an oil rig or in the circus? This is my answer to myself.

Mitchell has a [stammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stammer "Stammer").[^25] He believes that the film *[The King's Speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King%27s_Speech "The King's Speech")* (2010) is one of the most accurate portrayals of that experience for an individual.[^25] He said, "I'd probably still be avoiding the subject today had I not outed myself by writing a semi-autobiographical novel, *Black Swan Green*, narrated by a stammering 13-year-old." [^25] Mitchell is a patron of the [British Stammering Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Stammering_Association "British Stammering Association").[^26]

Mitchell's and Yoshida's son is severely autistic.[^27] Mitchell says that to be the parent of a child with autism, "you have to become a stronger, kinder, more compassionate, more patient, tougher, clear-sighted person", and it is accompanied by a gift, "real enlightenment about the human condition and the human heart".[^28] In 2013, they translated a book into English that was written by [Naoki Higashida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naoki_Higashida "Naoki Higashida"), a 13-year-old Japanese autistic boy, titled *[The Reason I Jump: One Boy's Voice from the Silence of Autism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reason_I_Jump:_One_Boy%27s_Voice_from_the_Silence_of_Autism "The Reason I Jump: One Boy's Voice from the Silence of Autism")*.[^29] Mitchell called the book "a revelatory godsend",[^28] providing both practical advice and leading him to think of their son having greater agency than he had previously thought, which helped their son.[^30] [^27] Initially, his wife translated parts of the book out loud for him at the kitchen table,[^27] and then they translated the book informally to give to their son's teachers and care-givers; however, Mitchell's agent and editor thought it might have a wider audience, leading to a formal translation.[^30]

In 2017, Mitchell and his wife translated a second book by Higashida, *[Fall Down 7 Times Get Up 8: A Young Man's Voice from the Silence of Autism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_Down_7_Times_Get_Up_8 "Fall Down 7 Times Get Up 8")*.[^31]
